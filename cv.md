# Suyash Gunjal

Physician, radiologist and clinical AI lead.

Public factual profile accompanying [su7sh.com](https://su7sh.com/).

I'm a radiologist who has led healthcare and education institutions serving rural and tribal communities in Maharashtra. I now lead clinicians evaluating medical AI at SpaceXSI.

## Work

### Leading rural care and education

As Managing Director and Chief Radiologist at the charitable Om Chaitanya Multi-specialty Hospital, I led work to broaden access to paediatric, neurology and urology care. I was responsible for clinical recruitment, hospital operations and regulatory work.

In the same rural setting, I also led Jaihind Nursing College and Shri J. R. Gunjal English Medium School & Junior College.

### Establishing a COVID centre

During the pandemic, I founded and led Jaihind Dedicated COVID Health Centre in Alephata, working through permissions, space, equipment, staffing and care. I then handed daily operations to a dedicated manager while completing my radiology residency.

At SMBT Charitable Hospital, my work included diagnostic imaging, pandemic-era triage, teaching, mentoring and rural health camps.

### Leading clinical AI evaluation

At SpaceXSI, I lead clinicians evaluating medical AI for accuracy, safety, handling of uncertainty and evidence-grounded reasoning. With medical experts and cross-functional teams, I work on review standards, evaluation quality and reviewer alignment.

My work includes clinical review frameworks, medical prompts and safety-oriented rubrics, with a focus on radiology.

## Approach and interests

In healthcare, I begin with the problem: who needs help, what gets in the way and what would count as a better result. Clinical requirements sit alongside questions about staffing, resources and how an institution works.

When AI could help, I consider what data and evaluation sets would be appropriate, what training data may be needed, and where human judgment remains necessary. The workflow, the people using it and the way its quality is assessed belong in the same discussion.

An idea also needs a path into use: understanding client needs, deciding what is worth building, organising delivery and considering who would adopt or pay for it. That is how I think about product and go-to-market decisions.

My interests in global health return to questions close to rural care: who can reach a service, whose needs shape it and whether it can be sustained locally.

Value-based healthcare asks what changes for patients and what resources that change requires. Environmental sustainability belongs in those decisions too, including how care uses materials and energy. These are questions I want to keep working on across clinical practice, institutions and technology.
